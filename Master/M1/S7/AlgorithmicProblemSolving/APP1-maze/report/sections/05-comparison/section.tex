\section{Comparison}
\label{sec:comparison}

Both solvers are implemented in C in \texttt{implementation/}, from the
pseudocode above, with a shared generator and no shared solving code. $200$
mazes per size, start $(0,0)$, exit at the far corner; every maze checked to be
a spanning tree, and both solvers checked to return the same cells in the same
order.

\begin{center}

  $32\times32$   & $1024$  & $180$  & $873$   & $738$   & $\mathbf{116}$ \\

\begin{description}[leftmargin=*,style=nextline,itemsep=5pt]
  \item[A* over Dijkstra: $4\%$.] A perfect maze has no rival routes, so the
    estimate has almost nothing to prune; A* still settles $82\%$ of the cells.
  \item[Tree over A*: a factor of $30$.] $1776$ against $53\,753$, and the gap
    widens with $n$: $\Theta(n^{0.68})$ against $\Theta(n)$. The tree search
    estimates nothing --- it knows where the walls are, having built them.
  \item[Memory.] A* allocates $g$, parent and settled for every cell before it
    starts, $\Theta(n)$. The tree search needs its recursion stack,
    $\Theta(\log n)$.
\end{description}
